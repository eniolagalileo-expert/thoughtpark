# ThoughtPark

A bedtime "brain dump" that parks your racing thoughts for the night, so your mind can stop looping, and hands them back to you in the morning.

## The Unique Kernel
You don't just write thoughts down; you **park** them. Each thought gets one quick decision: **"Tomorrow"** (becomes part of tomorrow's list) or **"Let it go"** (released for good). Once everything is parked, the screen goes quiet: nothing left to hold in your head tonight. In the morning, the "Tomorrow" thoughts are waiting as a short list.

(Agent's proposal, pending learner approval: sorting is a fast manual choice per thought rather than automatic AI sorting. Making the decision yourself is part of what settles the mind, and it keeps the proof of concept working with no API keys.)

## Who It's For
Someone lying in bed whose thoughts won't stop: replaying the day, worrying about tomorrow. The learner themselves: "When I can't sleep deeply and immediately," and what keeps them up is "my thoughts." Today they lie awake with the thoughts looping, or pick up their phone.

## The Core Loop
Night: open ThoughtPark → type each thought that's on your mind → tap "Tomorrow" or "Let it go" for each → see "All parked. Goodnight." → phone down.
Morning: open it → see last night's "Tomorrow" list, ready to act on.
Why they come back: the thoughts they parked were actually waiting for them, so it becomes safe to let them go at night.

## Inspiration & Identity
The learner asked for a mix of three ideas: a brain-dump pad (the core), a guided wind-down, and a sleep log. Feel: calm, dark, low-light night screens; minimal text; gentle animation when a thought is released. Nothing bright, busy, or gamified.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
A problem they have themselves: racing thoughts keep them from falling asleep quickly and sleeping deeply.

## What "Working" Looks Like
In a browser: type three thoughts, park two as "Tomorrow" and let one go (it gently fades away), reach the "All parked. Goodnight." screen, then switch to morning view and see the two "Tomorrow" thoughts waiting as a list. The "oh, that's cool" beat: the released thought dissolving, and the parked ones reappearing in the morning.

## The POC Boundary
- Night view: add thoughts, park each as Tomorrow or Let it go
- "All parked" goodnight screen
- Morning view: last night's Tomorrow list, with check-off
- Saved on the device between night and morning (no accounts)

## Later
- Guided 2-minute wind-down after parking (idea A)
- One-tap morning sleep rating and a view of which nights went well (idea C)
- Optional AI suggestions for sorting or rephrasing worries
- Voice input for thoughts

## Explicitly Cut
- Accounts, sync, and a backend: not needed to prove the kernel and add setup time.
- Automatic AI sorting: needs an API key and cost; the manual one-tap decision is part of the value.
- Sleep tracking with sensors/wearables: far outside a 2–4 hour proof of concept.
